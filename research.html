Research

Fast, reliable and physically consistent surrogate models for flows governed by PDEs

Many problems in science and engineering are described by partial differential equations (PDEs). Solving them accurately with high-fidelity numerical methods is often very costly, which becomes prohibitive when the same model must be run many times: for forecasting, optimization, control or uncertainty quantification (UQ). My research develops reduced-order models (ROMs) and surrogate models that make these computations fast while preserving accuracy and the underlying physics.

ROMs in a nutshell

The solutions of a PDE often live, in practice, close to a space of much lower dimension than the full numerical model. A ROM exploits this: it learns a small set of representative modes from high-fidelity solutions, then describes any new solution with only a few coefficients.

The computational effort is split into two stages:

  • Offline, done once: high-fidelity simulations are computed and compressed into a reduced basis, for instance by Proper Orthogonal Decomposition (POD).
  • Online, done many times: for a new parameter or scenario, only the few reduced coefficients are computed, which is orders of magnitude cheaper.

General principle of a ROM: a reduced basis is extracted offline, then the reduced dynamics are obtained either by projecting the equations (intrusive) or by learning them from data (non-intrusive).

Two families of approaches

Intrusive ROMs

The governing equations are projected onto the reduced basis (Galerkin-type methods). This requires access to the operators of the numerical solver, but keeps a direct link with the physics and allows error control.

Application: advection–diffusion problems.

Non-intrusive ROMs

The reduced dynamics are learned directly from simulation data, for instance with neural networks, without modifying the solver. This makes the approach usable with existing, complex or industrial codes.

Application: 2D shallow-water equations and flood simulation.

Application to floods

The two-dimensional shallow-water equations (SWE), also known as the Saint-Venant equations, describe free-surface flows in rivers and floodplains. They are central to flood forecasting, but their high-fidelity simulation is costly, and the solutions are hard to reduce: the dynamics are nonlinear and the wet/dry fronts move as the flood spreads.

My work builds non-intrusive ROMs for the 2D SWE that remain accurate on flood scenarios not seen during training, follow moving flood fronts, and respect physical quantities such as mass.

Main contributions

My work has addressed several difficulties that make floods hard to reduce:

  • Accuracy with few modes. By learning the POD projection error together with the reduced coefficients, the reduced model stays accurate with a small basis and compact neural networks, and was validated on real floods of the Aude river and the city of Abidjan.
  • Moving flood fronts. Registering the moving wetted area onto a fixed domain, combined with a global water-volume constraint, captures advancing fronts with far fewer modes and much smaller errors.
  • Changing flow regimes. Local ROMs, each adapted to a flow regime and selected online by a physics-guided classifier, improve accuracy when a single global model is not enough.
  • Speed for many-query studies. The resulting surrogates simulate whole flood events in seconds, making large ensembles and uncertainty quantification affordable.

Projects & collaborations

My PhD and my position as Teaching and Research Associate (2021–2026) were carried out at the Institut de Mathématiques de Toulouse (IMT), INSA Toulouse. During my PhD, I contributed to the ANR MUFFINS project, in collaboration with INRAE, on efficient simulation and model reduction for hydrodynamic applications.

I have worked with Jérôme Monnier (INSA Toulouse, IMT), Robin Bouclier (INSA Toulouse, Institut Clément Ader) and Pierre-André Garambois (INRAE, Aix-en-Provence).

Looking ahead

I want to extend physics-guided, non-intrusive model reduction to broader computational fluid dynamics. Directions I am particularly interested in:

  • Structure-preserving ROMs: reduced models that retain the key properties of the original equations, such as conservation laws, stability or energy balance.
  • Parametric and unsteady flows, with ROMs valid over wide ranges of physical parameters.
  • UQ, where fast surrogates make large ensembles of simulations affordable.
  • Coupling numerical solvers with learned surrogates, to combine the reliability of physics-based models with the speed of machine learning.

The common thread is ROMs that are fast, interpretable and reliable across flow regimes.

Beyond hydrodynamics, I am open to applying ROMs to other fields where high-fidelity simulations are a bottleneck, such as weather and climate modeling, ocean and atmospheric flows, environmental engineering, energy systems, or industrial fluid mechanics, and I welcome collaborations in these areas.
